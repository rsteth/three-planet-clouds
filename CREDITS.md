# Credits

Three Planet Clouds began as a cloud system for **ant-world**, created by Robin Stethem. The extraction retains its two quality levels, regional layout, worker bake, and tested composition pipeline. It builds on a long history of publicly explained real-time rendering techniques.

## Research and rendering foundations

- **Andrew Schneider and the Guerrilla team — [Nubis, Evolved](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-evolved)**. A useful reference for envelope-driven cloud modeling and avoiding temporal artifacts. This small renderer is not a reproduction of the Nubis system.
- **Ken Perlin — [Improving Noise](https://mrl.cs.nyu.edu/~perlin/paper445.pdf), 2002**. Gradient noise and the quintic interpolation curve.
- **Steven Worley — [A Cellular Texture Basis Function](https://doi.org/10.1145/237170.237267), 1996**. Nearest-feature cellular noise.
- **L. G. Henyey and J. L. Greenstein**, 1941. The anisotropic phase function used here for a compact approximation of forward scattering.
- **Robert Ulichney — [The Void-and-Cluster Method for Dither Array Generation](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.152707), 1993**. The rank-mask technique behind fixed spatial sample jitter. The included bytes come from ant-world; see [Provenance](docs/PROVENANCE.md) for the limits of the retained generation record.
- **Three.js authors and contributors — [Three.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/r169)**. Scene graph, instancing, shader chunks, texture/resource management, and the ACES/output conventions used by the renderer.
- **Timothy Lottes / NVIDIA**, and the compact WebGL FXAA lineage maintained by **Armin Ronacher** and **Matt DesLauriers**. The output shader uses the familiar diagonal-luma direction and two candidate filters. [glsl-fxaa](https://github.com/mattdesl/glsl-fxaa) supplies the retained compact-filter notices.
- **Stephen Hill**, with **Eric Heitz, Jonathan Dupuy, and David Neubelt**, for the fitted ACES transform lineage referenced by Three.js. The constants used to invert the authored clear color match that fitted transform. See the [upstream shader](https://github.com/selfshadow/ltc_code/blob/master/webgl/shaders/ltc/ltc_blit.fs).
- As requested by the retained LTC source license: **Real-Time Polygonal-Light Shading with Linearly Transformed Cosines**, Eric Heitz, Jonathan Dupuy, Stephen Hill, and David Neubelt, ACM Transactions on Graphics 35(4), 2016. [Project page](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/415-2/). This release uses the tone-transform lineage, not the LTC area-light algorithm.
